MagicBit (esp32/esp32/magicbit) — button/LED research notes
=============================================================

Sources consulted
------------------
https://magicbit-arduino.readthedocs.io/en/stable/
  - "What is Magicbit" overview: confirms two push-buttons on the front,
    four onboard LEDs (red, yellow, green, blue) on the backside, plus
    buzzer, potentiometer, LDR, OLED, motor driver (all out of scope).

https://magicbit-arduino.readthedocs.io/en/latest/
  - Confirms same board layout/description as stable docs; example code
    fetched shows `pinMode(18,OUTPUT); digitalWrite(18,HIGH); // turns on
    LED` for the yellow LED, and a button-reading example:
    `pinMode(34, INPUT); digitalRead(34);` with text stating "Buttons on
    the board are pulled up internally ... pressed = LOW (0), not pressed
    = HIGH (1)". Confirms buttons are simple momentary pushbuttons, not
    something else.

https://www.espboards.dev/esp32/magicbit/
  - Pinout table: RED LED=GPIO27, YELLOW LED=GPIO18, GREEN LED=GPIO16,
    BLUE LED=GPIO17, LEFT BUTTON=GPIO35, RIGHT BUTTON=GPIO34,
    LED_BUILTIN=GPIO16 (same pin as GREEN LED). Matches this variant's
    pins_arduino.h exactly (RED_LED=27, YELLOW_LED=18, GREEN_LED=16,
    BLUE_LED=17, RIGHT_PUTTON=34, LEFT_BUTTON=35, LED_BUILTIN=16).

WebSearch results (magicbitlk/Magicbit-Arduino library, Hackster.io
"Magicbit: An Easy IOT Platform for Everyone" project writeup)
  - Reiterated: "There are two buttons on the front of the Magicbit" and
    "There are four leds on backside of the Magicbit with color red,
    yellow, green & blue." Example digitalWrite(27,HIGH) turns on
    RED_LED confirming active-high polarity for the discrete LEDs.

Conclusion: these are four INDIVIDUAL discrete LEDs (red/yellow/green/
blue), NOT a single-data-pin NeoPixel/addressable RGB strip and NOT a
3-wire discrete RGB LED (R/G/B on one package) — they are four separate,
independently-colored single LEDs on four separate GPIOs. The task brief
mentioned "onboard NeoPixel/RGB LED" as a possibility, but real vendor
documentation does not support a NeoPixel/addressable LED on this board;
tagged them as ordinary active-high LEDs (led_high) instead, per the
actual confirmed hardware.

Pins considered but NOT tagged
-------------------------------
GPIO16 (GREEN_LED, also aliased as LED_BUILTIN) - confirmed real onboard
  active-high discrete LED via both magicbit-arduino docs and espboards.dev.
  NOT TAGGED because GPIO16 falls inside the chip-level PSRAM pin-exclusion
  range (16-17) and has no chipGpios entry at all in the base esp32 board.json
  (see variants/esp32/esp32/board.json chipGpios list, which jumps from 15
  straight to 18). Re-adding GPIO16 to chipGpios would reverse a pre-existing
  chip-level exclusion from an earlier audit pass, which is explicitly out of
  scope for this task. This is a confirmed LED, not a guess — flagging for
  human review in case the exclusion should be reconsidered for this specific
  module variant (some MagicBit revisions may not have onboard PSRAM, but no
  vendor documentation was found confirming PSRAM is or isn't present on this
  module, so I made no guess either way and left it untagged).

GPIO17 (BLUE_LED) - same situation as GPIO16: confirmed real onboard
  active-high discrete LED, but excluded from chipGpios for the same PSRAM
  pin-exclusion reason. Not tagged, for human review.

No TX/RX conflicts were found: this board's TX=1, RX=3 (pins_arduino.h),
neither of which coincide with any candidate button/LED pin (16, 17, 18,
27, 34, 35).

What was tagged
-----------------
GPIO27 -> ["digital_output","led_high","pwm_output"] (RED_LED, active-high,
  pwm_output retained because it was already present on this pin).
GPIO18 -> ["digital_output","led_high","pwm_output"] (YELLOW_LED, active-high,
  pwm_output retained because it was already present on this pin).
GPIO34 -> ["digital_input","button"] (RIGHT_PUTTON, i.e. right push button).
GPIO35 -> ["digital_input","button"] (LEFT_BUTTON, i.e. left push button).
