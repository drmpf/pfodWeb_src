https://files.waveshare.com/wiki/ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-2.8/ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-2.8.pdf (official vendor schematic, linked from the docs.waveshare.com "Resources-And-Documents" page)
  - THE decisive source. Full schematic text was extracted (component
    pin lists + flat netlist dump). Key findings:
    * Three physical switches: Key1, Key2, Key3.
    * Key2's signal pin ties directly to net "IO0"/"GPIO0", alongside
      pull-up resistor R20 (10K) and debounce cap C28, and directly to
      U5 (ESP32-S3R8) physical pin 5, which the same schematic's own
      U5 pin table labels "GPIO0 5". Key2's other pin ties to GND
      (confirmed in the GND net's member list). This is the BOOT
      button, wired to GPIO0 - standard ESP32-S3 BOOT-strap topology.
    * Key3's signal pin ties directly to net "RESET", which traces to
      U5 pin "CHIP_PU 4" (the chip's EN/reset pin, not a GPIO). This is
      the RESET button - not software-readable, not taggable.
    * Key1's signal path runs through diodes D2/D4 and P-MOSFET Q1 into
      nets explicitly labelled "Key_BAT" and "BAT_Control" in the
      schematic's own GPIO/LCD/SD Card/IMU/RTC/UART/I2C/Speak/"Other"
      pin table: IO6 -> "Key_BAT" (Other column), IO7 -> "BAT_Control"
      (Other column, net "NLIO7 NLBAT0Control" in the netlist dump).
      This is the "Battery Power Control Button" - Key1 is the physical
      pushbutton, GPIO6 is how firmware reads its press state
      (Key_BAT), GPIO7 is the associated power-hold/enable output
      (BAT_Control).
    * LED1 ("Charging indicator", silkscreen "Red"): netlist shows its
      cathode tied to U1 pin 9, and U1's own pin table (component
      "ETA6098" battery-charger IC) labels pin 9 "STAT". So LED1 is
      hardwired to the charger IC's status output pin, not a
      general-purpose ESP32 GPIO - cannot be tagged (not even a chip
      GPIO net).
    * LED2 ("Power indicator", silkscreen "Red"): wired directly across
      the 3V3/USB_5V supply rail through resistor R28 (2.2K) - always
      on when powered, not GPIO-controlled, not taggable.
    * No WS2812/NeoPixel/APA2068-style addressable-RGB LED component
      appears anywhere in the schematic text (APA2068KAI-TRG present
      is an audio power amplifier, unrelated to LEDs, despite the
      superficially similar part-number pattern to "APA102"-style LED
      driver chips - verified it is wired to the speaker/audio-out
      nets, not any LED).
    * Confirmed pins_arduino.h's TX=43/RX=44/SCL=10/SDA=11 all match
      the schematic's own "Other"/I2C table exactly, and GPIO8 in the
      schematic's Other column is explicitly "BAT_ADC" (battery voltage
      ADC sense), not any LED or button - this directly contradicts a
      third-party claim (see below) and was treated as authoritative.

PINS CONSIDERED BUT NOT TAGGED
-------------------------------
- GPIO7 (BAT_Control): confirmed real signal (power-hold/enable output
  paired with the GPIO6 Key1 button), but it is an output line the
  firmware drives, not a button or LED itself. Left untagged. This is
  a confident judgment call based on direct schematic evidence, not a
  guess.
- RESET button (Key3): ties to the chip's CHIP_PU/EN pin, which has no
  chipGpios entry at all (not a GPIO). Cannot be tagged - out of scope,
  not a guess, simply not a taggable pin.
- LED1 "Charging indicator": hardwired to the ETA6098 charger IC's
  STAT pin, not any ESP32 GPIO. Cannot be tagged - confirmed via
  schematic, not a guess.
- LED2 "Power indicator": hardwired directly across the power rail,
  always on, not GPIO-controlled. Cannot be tagged - confirmed via
  schematic, not a guess.
- GPIO8: considered because of the (rejected) third-party "RGB LED on
  GPIO8" claim. The official schematic shows GPIO8 = BAT_ADC (an
  analog battery-voltage sense input), not an LED. NOT tagged - this
  was a deliberate rejection of an unreliable source, not a guess left
  for human review; confidence is high that GPIO8 is not an LED.
- No NeoPixel/WS2812/discrete RGB LED found anywhere on this board;
  none tagged.
