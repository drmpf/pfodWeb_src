PINS CONSIDERED AND NOT TAGGED:
- GPIO5: WebSearch AI summary suggested this as a "programmable LED" pin, but the vendor wiki's own pinout
  table shows GPIO5 with no special function (plain "GPIO5", no KEY_IO/LED annotation), and the Onboard
  Resources photo-caption list (items 1-16) has no LED item at all. This is a clear case where I made NO
  guess — the WebSearch summary is not trusted as a "real vendor documentation" source per task rules, and
  the primary wiki page itself does not corroborate it. Left GPIO5 with its existing/default capabilities
  in board.json, untouched.
- GPIO9 (Charge indicator LED, item 9) and the pin behind "Power indicator" (item 11): both are fixed status
  indicator LEDs shown in the Onboard Resources photo captions, but the pinout table does not associate
  either with a GPIO row (no matching GPIO entry for "charge" or "power" indicator) — they appear to be
  hardwired to the battery-charge IC / VBUS rather than driven by an ESP32 GPIO the firmware can control.
  Not tagged; no GPIO candidate identified for them at all, so no judgment call was needed here.
- EN pin: pinout table lists "RESET" against EN, i.e. the RESET button is wired to the chip's hardware EN
  pin, not a GPIO. EN is not a GPIO in this codebase's chipGpios map, so nothing to tag.
- No TX/RX conflict arose: GPIO0 (the confirmed BOOT button) is not this board's TX (43) or RX (44) pin, so
  the tag was safe to apply.

WHAT WAS TAGGED:
- GPIO0 capabilities changed from ["digital_input","digital_output","pwm_output"] to
  ["digital_input","button"] (onboard BOOT button, confirmed via vendor wiki KEY_IO=BOOT0 pinout entry).
  No onboard LED was tagged — none could be confirmed via real vendor documentation.
